Writing DECtalk songs with an RNN

October 2024 · Machine learning, Software

A course project for CMPUT 652, Procedural Content Generation via Machine Learning, at the University of Alberta.

DECtalk is a speech synthesizer from the 1980s, and it can also sing. You give it phonemes, each with a length and a pitch, and it holds each sound for that long on that note. People have written a lot of songs for it this way. I train a recurrent neural network on about a thousand of these song files and have it write new ones. The idea comes from the video AI learns to sing CREEPY music. The code, the trained weights and the samples are on GitHub.

The song format

A DECtalk song is a plain text file. This is the start of Deck the Halls from the training set:

[:PHONE ON] [:nv] [:ra 180] [:dv hs 95 br 0 as 90 ap 90 gn 75]
[DEH<350,25>K<150>DHAX<150,23>HXAO<150,22>LX<100>Z<50>
WIH<200,20>TH<100>BAW<200,18>Z<100>AH<200,20>V<100>HXAO<300,22>LIY<300,18>
FAO<150,20>LAO<150,22>LAO<150,23>LAO<125,20>LAO<450,22>LAO<150,20>
LAO<300,18>LAO<300,17>LAO<450,18>_<150>

The bracketed commands at the top set things up. [:PHONE ON] turns on phoneme input, [:nv] picks one of DECtalk’s voices, [:ra 180] sets the speaking rate, and [:dv ...] tweaks the voice’s settings. After that come the phonemes. DEH<350,25> is the “de” in “deck”, held for 350 ms at pitch 25. Consonants at the end of a syllable usually get a length and no pitch, and _ is a rest. The run of LAO is “fa la la la la la la la la”.

The data

The training songs come from theflameofhope.co, which has pages of DECtalk songs sorted by category. A short script, scrape.py, downloads every .txt file linked from the Christmas, children’s, country, gospel and rock pages. That gives 1,085 songs, everything from hymns and worship songs to Christmas carols and DECtalk versions of Madonna and Beatles songs. Joined into one string, that’s about 4.3 million characters, drawn from 81 distinct characters.

The model

The network is a character-level LSTM in PyTorch. It reads the song text one character at a time and predicts the next one. I don’t tell it anything about phonemes or pitches. To the model, DEH<350,25> is just eleven characters in a row.

InputOne-hot vector over the 81 characters
Network2 LSTM layers of 512 units with dropout 0.5, then a linear layer back to 81 outputs
ParametersAbout 3.4 million
TrainingText split into 64 parallel streams and fed in 70-character chunks, cross-entropy loss, Adam at a learning rate of 0.001, gradient norm clipped at 5, 20 epochs
SamplingStart from [:, temperature 0.8, 1,000 characters

At each step the network takes the current character as a one-hot vector \(\mathbf{x}_t\), updates its state and scores every possible next character:

\[ \mathbf{h}_t = \operatorname{LSTM}(\mathbf{x}_t,\ \mathbf{h}_{t-1}), \qquad \mathbf{z}_t = W\mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{b} . \]

Here \(\mathbf{h}_t\) stands for the state of both layers, and \(W\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are the linear layer applied to the top layer’s output. Training minimizes the cross-entropy between the softmax of \(\mathbf{z}_t\) and the character that actually comes next. To generate a song, I feed in [:, which is how every DECtalk command starts, and then sample each next character from

\[ p(c_{t+1} = i) = \frac{\exp(z_{t,i}/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_{t,j}/T)} \]

with temperature \(T = 0.8\). The sampled character goes back in as the next input. A temperature below 1 shifts probability toward the characters the model already rates highest.

What it writes

In every sample the brackets and the <length,pitch> tags open and close in pairs, except at the very end, where the 1,000 characters run out partway through a phoneme. The samples run between 23 and 53 seconds. In all twenty I’ve kept, the settings line at the top appears word for word in the training songs. The one in the sample below, [:nv] [:ra 180] [:dv hs 95 br 0 as 90 ap 90 gn 75], shows up in 412 of the 1,085 songs. Here’s the start of that sample, the one at the top of the post, with line breaks added:

[:nv] [:ra 180] [:dv hs 95 br 0 as 90 ap 90 gn 75]
[AY<200,25>MAH<125,22>ST<75>LAY<300,25>F<100>IH<300,20>Z<100>
YXOR<200>LAH<125,18>V<75>IH<125>Z<75>TUW<200,20>MIY<400,20>_<800>
YU<200,22>HXAE<125,20>V<75>GAO<125,27>T<75>LIY<200,25>SIY<400,27>
DXIH<125>D<75>SAO<125>NX<75>IH<125>N<75>MAY<200,25>HXAR<125,25>T<75>

Read out, that’s roughly “I must life is your love is to me. You have got lee seeded song in my heart.”

Another sample has a line made of 48 “la”s with one rest in the middle.
