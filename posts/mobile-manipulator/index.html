A haptic mobile manipulator

May 26, 2024 · Teleoperation, Haptics, Machining, Computer vision, Electronics, Software

2022–2024. Undergraduate research assistant on a lab team.

This is a teleoperated robot for disaster zones and other places too dangerous to send a person. A 7‑DOF Barrett WAM rides on a Clearpath Husky. The operator drives it from a second WAM fitted with a cable-driven wrist exoskeleton and feels the forces the robot meets. A 360° camera on the robot feeds a VR headset. We demonstrate it to the Department of National Defence as one of eight finalist teams in the IDEaS (Innovation for Defence Excellence and Security) challenge, which funds it with a $500K grant.

Haptic teleoperation

The robot’s arm is a 7‑DOF WAM. The operator side is a 4‑DOF WAM with our 3‑DOF cable-driven wrist exoskeleton on the end, which matches the robot’s seven joints one for one. The wrist is light and runs on low-cost BLDC motor controllers. Because it’s cable driven, joint torque is a linear function of motor output, so the force the operator feels is scaled correctly.

The two arms are coupled joint to joint. Each joint runs a PD position loop with the matching joint on the other arm as its setpoint, so when the robot pushes on something, the operator’s arm pushes back. Either arm can act as the leader. How stiff we can make that coupling and still keep it stable depends on the communication latency between the two arms.

The mobile base

In the concept video the WAM sits on top of the Husky. For the real robot I redesign and build the Husky’s chassis and body panels so the WAM can sit lower, which brings the robot’s centre of mass down. The WAM mounts to the chassis through milled aluminum parts.

Cameras and the headset

The operator sees the scene through a FLIR Ladybug2 spherical camera on a mast. It captures 360° images at 10 Hz and sends them to the operator over UDP. We project each spherical image onto a virtual cube and show it in an Oculus Rift headset. Two FLIR Blackfly cameras give higher-resolution views to the front and rear.

Mapping, ROS and Wi‑Fi

I integrate the sensors in ROS: the LiDAR, the Ladybug2 and Blackfly cameras, an IMU and GPS, with URDF models of the whole robot. The LiDAR is a Hokuyo UTM‑30LX‑EW, and with SLAM on it the robot can map a floor and drive itself around it. I also get some legacy LiDAR hardware running on ROS Noetic, tracking down hardware and communication faults with an oscilloscope and serial diagnostics.

We also test the robot’s long-range Wi‑Fi outside.

The IDEaS demonstration

For the IDEaS demonstration we run a search-and-rescue scenario with five tasks: remote tabletop manipulation, zero-tolerance precision manipulation, remote door opening, autonomous room navigation and interactive scene awareness. I help demonstrate it to DND. Here’s the door task in our lab:
