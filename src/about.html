About

I’m an MSc student in Martin Jagersand’s Computer Vision and Robotics Group at the University of Alberta, finishing in December 2026. Before that I did a BSc there in Mathematics and Computing Science. In summer 2026 I worked at Barrett Technology in Boston, where I engineered the Alberta wrist, a new haptic wrist for their WAM arm.

Most of my projects need software, electronics, control and mechanical design all at once, and that mix is what I like about them. A lot of my research has been on haptic devices for teleoperation. I’ve also built mobile and legged robots, a painting robot, a panoramic camera, generative models, and software from motor test tools to a Mac app.

What I work on

  • Embedded systems and real-time control. C++ control loops at 500 Hz over CANopen, firmware for Teensy boards, motor drives, and the test suite I use to measure what a device can do.
  • Software, simulation and machine learning. Python and C++, MuJoCo models of a hopping robot and a quadruped, reinforcement learning for the quadruped’s backflip, generative models, computer vision, and a Mac app in Swift.
  • Robotics, haptics and teleoperation. Six haptic wrists for the Barrett WAM, leader–follower teleoperation of two WAMs, and a mobile manipulator.
  • Mechanical design and machining. Cable drives and differentials, FEA, and parts I machine myself on CNC mills and lathes. The WHAM wrist is my biggest machining project so far.
